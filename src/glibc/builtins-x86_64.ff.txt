    The SSE builtins of gcc that x86_64's string functions use (strcspn-sse4.c, strspn-sse4.c: <nmmintrin.h>), each a C function of vectors (vector / char / 16, gcc's __v16qi)
    that gives their addresses to an asm function of its own (rdi rsi, the result's rdx), which loads them into xmm0 and xmm1.
    __builtin_ia32_pmovmskb128 x: pmovmskb, a bit of each byte's top. __builtin_ia32_pshufb128 x y: pshufb, x's bytes by y's indexes.
    __builtin_ia32_pcmpistr<i ic iz>128 <imm> x y: pcmpistri $imm, its index (ecx), its CF, its ZF (one for each immediate glibc uses).

    gcc's __builtin_sqrt and __builtin_sqrtf on x86_64 (-fno-math-errno, as glibc's libm is built): sqrtsd and sqrtss, the root rounded once, as gcc makes them;
    each a C function that gives the addresses of its operand and its result (rdi rsi) to an asm function of its own.
    __builtin_fma and __builtin_fmaf: gcc calls fma and fmaf, libm's (s_fma.c, s_fmaf.c: their ifuncs pick FMA3, FMA4 or the C of SSE2), when FMA is not on (glibc is built for x86-64 without it); so do these.
