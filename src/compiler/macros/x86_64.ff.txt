c:
  comment
    The C layer of ttpc, for x86_64: the primitives it emits, in its own terms. V is the value (rax), L the left operand (r10), X a scale or count (r11), FP the frame (rbp), SP the stack (rsp).
    One slot of the stack is 8 bytes. Every primitive has one fixed size.
    The divisions and the shifts need rdx and rcx too: they keep them on the stack, so C changes no register but rax, r10, r11 (and what a call or a system call changes: syscall changes rcx and r11).
    x86 traps on a division by zero and on the most negative number / -1, where riscv64 gives a value.

rv64:
  comment
    rv64: ttpc's own instructions, RV64 in ff order (the destination last), in x86_64 instructions: what ttpc, written in RV64, is assembled with to run on x86_64.
    mkhost.pl renames each op of ttpc to rv.op and each register to rv.reg; mkwhole.pl puts this group in the whole tree of ttpc for the seed.
    The RV64 registers are slots of 8 bytes in memory, rbx holds their address: x86_64 has too few registers, and the same slots for all of them keep every instruction one fixed size.
    These are the forms with every register in its slot; the ten most used live in x86_64 registers of their own, in the forms rv.op.h... of rv64alu: rv64mem: rv64jump: (below, made by gen/mkrv64x86.pl), which mkhost.pl picks.
    rax rcx rdx are the scratch registers, rsp is the call stack of call and ret, ra a slot that nothing reads. mkhost.pl names them rv.a0 ... (x86_64 has an sp of its own).
    rv.start regs stack: first in _start, rbx = regs, the slot of sp = rsp (argc and argv stay at 0(sp) and 8(sp) ...), rsp = stack, the end of the call stack.
